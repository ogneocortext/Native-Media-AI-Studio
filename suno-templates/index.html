5 · Where mini stops

The templates get the best draft mini can give. Everything past this line belongs to Native Media AI Studio — not to a longer prompt, and not to a Pro upgrade.

Consistency passMini has no Max Mode, and its arrangement control drifts over a long song. Locking a keeper's structure down is a studio pass, not another reroll.
Mix polishMini output is draft-grade. Loudness, stereo width, and the final master finish happen in the studio.
Voice identityThere are no Persona or Voice tools on the free tier, so a recurring vocal character across tracks is built in the studio, not prompted into mini.
Arrangement repairFewer decisions per prompt reduces drift; what still wanders gets repaired downstream instead of chased with a longer prompt.

A keeper goes two places: straight into the bike-ride rotation as-is, or into the studio for the consistency and mix pass.

Keep the experiment legible

  • Batching is recommended, but each round should test one changed variable.
  • Target 200–350 lyric words for a full song; dense sheets can be rushed or skipped.
  • Use line breaks as breaths. Parentheses may become backing vocals, so use them sparingly.
  • End with [Outro] plus [Fade Out] or [End].
  • Set Variety to 0 so mini does not rewrite an engineered prompt. Its free-tier availability is not confirmed.
  • Mini follows fewer decisions than flagship v6: if a take drifts, shorten the prompt before adding more direction. Complexity is a liability on mini.
  • Direct the intro explicitly — adjectives describe the intro, instructions describe the song; the first seconds are where cheesy takes happen.
  • Performance verbs beat emotion words: belted, deadpan, snarled steer mini; heartfelt and passionate barely do.
