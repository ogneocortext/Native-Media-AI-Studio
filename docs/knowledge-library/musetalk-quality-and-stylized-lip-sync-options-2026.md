> [!info] Purpose
> Decision record for lip-sync quality on stylized/illustrated avatars.
> Captures the measured MuseTalk v1.5 result on the neon cyberpunk portrait,
> the mechanism that caps mouth quality, and the researched option space
> (model swaps, parameter tuning, post-processing, and style-preserving
> recomposites) for the next render.

---

## Bottom line

MuseTalk v1.5 is the right *local, real-time, MIT-licensed* lip-sync engine
for this machine, but it is architecturally capped at a **256×256 face crop**
that regenerates the lower face and composites it back through a semantic
mask. On a photoreal frontal face that trade is acceptable; on an illustrated
cyberpunk portrait the realism prior turns the mouth into a soft blob. The
next "better version" is therefore not a knob tweak — it is a choice between:

1. **accept MuseTalk's mouth and post-process it** (USM sharpen; GFPGAN/
   CodeFormer are risky on anime),
2. **preserve the illustration by syncing only the mouth region and
   recompositing it** (warp/re-dub + feathered mask, or a 2D mouth-shape
   pipeline), or
3. **change the input/avatar or the model class** (photoreal avatar,
   heavier lip-sync model, or a purpose-built anime pipeline).

## What we measured (2026-11-10 smoke render)

Render: `output/video/maya_clone_lipsync_smoke_v2.mp4` — job
`f4935731-0314-47b7-8f4b-42b11fcbeb9f`, 8.28 s, 1254×1254, 25 fps, H.264+AAC,
585,292 B. Input: `Neon Cat-Ear Cyberpunk Portrait.png` + cloned-voice MP3.

Vision QA (`qwen3.5:4b` on a 4×4 mouth contact sheet) scored:

| Dimension | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lip-sync plausibility | 2/10 | Mouth held a soft "O"-like shape; little vowel/consonant articulation |
| Artifact severity | 7/10 | Blurry edges, washed-out interior, no clear teeth/tongue |
| Temporal variety | 1/10 | Mouth shapes nearly identical across 8 s |
| Identity preservation | 8/10 | Character design stayed consistent outside the mouth |

Pixel-diff checks (mouth ROI `590,575,210,150` vs background):

- mouth-region mean abs diff **2.03/255**, nonzero on **89.3%** of frames —
  motion is real but low-amplitude.
- lower-face ROI mean **0.97**, whole-face ROI **0.34**, background **0.00** —
  MuseTalk only rewrites the lower face, as designed.
- mouth ROI vs original still: **46.7%** of pixels changed; whole face crop:
  **5.1%**.
- audio-envelope correlation peaked **≈0.51** at +3 frames (~0.12 s lead);
  onset correlation ≈0 — sync is present but mushy, not crisp. This is a
  proxy metric, not phoneme-level ground truth.

Original-vs-rendered mouth compare (`qwen3.5:4b`): the original had defined,
glossy lips; the render replaced them with an amorphous pink/purple blur with
no teeth/tongue and a soft boundary. A `gemma4:e2b-it-qat` face-level compare
was more lenient (identity/clothes/hair preserved, "mouth moved into a
slightly open shape") — trust the mouth-crop comparison; it isolates the
region that actually degraded.

USM post-pass test (`ffmpeg unsharp=5:5:0.8`): improved lip-edge definition
with no visible halos, but only ~0.35% of mouth pixels changed. Cheap and
safe; not a fix for the underlying blur.

## Why MuseTalk does this

Mechanism (from `D:\MuseTalk` source + README):

- Face crop is resized to **256×256** before the UNet (`scripts/inference.py`
  `crop_frame = cv2.resize(crop_frame, (256,256))`), then the decoded result
  is resized back to the bbox and pasted through `get_image()`
  (`musetalk/utils/blending.py`).
- For `--version v15`, `bbox_shift` is forced to 0; the controllable knobs are
  `extra_margin`, `parsing_mode`, `left/right_cheek_width`,
  `audio_padding_length_left/right`, `batch_size`, `fps`, `use_float16`,
  `use_saved_coord` (`scripts/inference.py` argparse).
- `parsing_mode="jaw"` (default) builds the paste-back mask from BiSeNet face
  parsing classes — skin around the jaw plus mouth classes 11–13, nose
  excluded, cheeks protected by `*_cheek_width`
  (`musetalk/utils/face_parsing/__init__.py`). Only that mask region is
  replaced; the upper face, hair, and background come from the original.
- README limitations state it plainly: 256×256 face region, single-frame
  generation → temporal jitter, lower-half synthesis → mustache/lip
  shape/color identity loss, and it recommends GFPGAN for higher resolution.
- Independent issue reports match: blurry mouth/bounding box is a known
  latent-space artifact (MuseTalk issue #335); WIDER-lipSync measured MuseTalk
  identity 0.789 vs LatentSync 0.959 and ~2× mouth LPIPS distortion, though
  MuseTalk had *less* temporal jitter.

Consequence for our avatar: the mouth is regenerated by a realism prior that
was not trained on this illustration style, then upscaled ~1.8× back into a
~395×497 px face crop. fp16→fp32 and `extra_margin` do not change that cap.

Local integration note: our S3FD detector patch
(`D:\MuseTalk\musetalk\utils\face_detection\api.py`) downscales detection input
to ≤1152 px and rescales boxes back, which fixed the >1152 px no-detection →
`division by zero` failure. That is a detection fix, unrelated to mouth
quality.

## MuseTalk knobs — what they can and cannot do

| Knob | Effect | Helps our defect? |
|---|---|---|
| `use_float16` off (fp32) | Slightly more precision, ~2× VRAM/time | Unlikely to fix blur; README/quality guides say fp32 not proven sharper. Try only for a final pass if VRAM is free. |
| `extra_margin` (default 10, max ~40) | Extends lower face crop (chin/neck) for v15 | May hide jaw seams; does not sharpen the mouth itself. |
| `parsing_mode` (`jaw`/`raw`/`neck`) | Changes paste-back mask area | `jaw` is the standard; `raw`/`neck` paste *more* synthesized skin — probably worse for us. |
| `left/right_cheek_width` (default 90) | Cheek protection width in jaw mask | Tweak only if cheek composite floats. |
| `audio_padding_length_left/right` (default 2) | Whisper feature context frames before/after each frame | Only lever that touches sync onset/offset; our onset corr ≈0 makes this worth one A/B, not a quality fix. |
| `batch_size` | Throughput only | No quality effect at 1 on 8 GB. |
| `fps` | Output rate for still input | Keep 25 (training fps). |
| `use_saved_coord` / `--saved_coord` | Reuse face bbox pickle | Speed only; coords already fine after detector patch. |
| `bbox_shift` | Mouth openness shift | **v1 only** — v15 forces 0. Not available to us. |

Wrapper gap: `tools/musetalk_run.py` currently forwards only
`--batch-size`, `--fps`, and the fp16 flag. `parsing_mode`, `extra_margin`,
cheek widths, and audio padding require either wrapper args or a direct
`scripts.inference` call. `config/lipsync.json` → `inference.command` is the
place to add literal args for a one-off render; per-job exposure would need
adapter/API params.

## Option space (researched 2026-11)

| Option | Input | VRAM / hardware | License | Stylized fit | Verdict for us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MuseTalk 1.5 (current)** | video/still + audio | ~8 GB fp16, 30 fps+ on V100; ran ~4.5 min for 8 s on 1070 Ti | MIT, commercial OK | Poor–fair; realism prior blurs illustrated mouths | Keep as baseline; tune + post only if accepting the look |
| **MuseTalk 1.0 (`--version v1`)** | same | similar | MIT | Same 256 cap; `bbox_shift` available for mouth openness | We have `models/musetalk` weights; worth one A/B only if v15 mouth openness is the complaint, not blur |
| **LatentSync 1.5** | video + audio | ~8–10 GB — borderline on 8 GB 1070 Ti | Apache-2.0 code; InsightFace models non-commercial research | Still 256 face; diffusion realism prior can freeze painted mouths | Better sync/identity on paper (WIDER-lipSync), but VRAM-risky and same stylized-domain problem |
| **LatentSync 1.6** | video + audio | ~18 GB | same | 512×512 face — sharper mouth | Not feasible on GTX 1070 Ti |
| **Wav2Lip / Wav2Lip+GFPGAN** | video + audio | light | README: **non-commercial only** | Warps low-res mouth instead of re-rendering — gentler on painted texture | License blocks commercial use; useful as a *technique* reference for mouth-crop warp |
| **SadTalker** | still + audio | medium; tested on M1 Mac | Apache-2.0 | Docs: works on real people / near-real portraits; anime support "will be released" — not there | Skip for this avatar |
| **InfiniteTalk** | video or image + audio | low-VRAM mode exists but Wan-2.1-based — likely >8 GB practical | Apache-2.0 | Moves head+body, not just mouth | Too heavy for routine 8 GB lip-sync |
| **LivePortrait** | portrait + driving video/template | 6–8 GB (min ~4 GB) | code MIT, weights disputed | Not audio-driven; needs a driving performance | Wrong tool for audio→lip-sync |
| **Rhubarb Lip Sync** | audio only | CPU, tiny | open-source CLI | Purpose-built for 2D: emits mouth shapes A–F (+G/H/X) timed to phonemes | Right *shape vocabulary* for anime, but needs mouth-shape assets/driver — not a drop-in renderer |
| **anime-lipsync (ComfyUI node)** | frames + audio | diffusion pipeline (Z-Image/Qwen + ControlNet) — heavy, needs ComfyUI up | repo public, 0★ — vet before trusting | Purpose-built: YOLO face+mouth → Allosaurus phonemes → 3 mouth types → ControlNet generation → BEN2/rembg alpha → feathered recomposite | Most style-native option found; ComfyUI is currently offline and deps are non-trivial |
| **MagicInfinite** | image + audio | ~48 GB (12 GB w/ offload); 8×H100 demos | public demo | Explicitly supports anime/cartoon | Not feasible locally |
| **Hosted lip-sync (e.g. sync-3, Pikaformance)** | video/still + audio | n/a | paid | Varies | Violates local-only constraint (D42) — excluded |

Key sourced facts:

- MuseTalk paper/README: 256×256 face region, 30 fps+ on V100, MIT license,
  recommends GFPGAN for resolution; lower-half synthesis loses lip
  shape/color identity.
- LatentSync: 1.5 ≈8 GB/256 px, 1.6 ≈18 GB/512 px; better LSE-C sync than
  MuseTalk but heavier; InsightFace components are non-commercial research.
- WIDER-lipSync (independent eval): LatentSync identity 0.959–0.960, mouth
  LPIPS 0.106; MuseTalk identity 0.786–0.789, mouth LPIPS ~0.20; MuseTalk
  smoother temporally but less faithful.
- Stylized-character practice (moltbook/facefusion-derived): for painted
  plates the realism prior is the enemy — crop the mouth, sync low-res, and
  recomposite over the untouched plate; feather the mask ~8–15 px, track to
  nose/chin, blend ~70–85%, match grain last.
- GFPGAN/CodeFormer: strong on real faces; community reports they
  "humanize"/distort anime and line art — use only region-limited and
  low-strength if at all.
- Rhubarb: Hanna-Barbera 6-shape mouth chart (A–F) + optional G/H/X; CLI
  outputs TSV/XML/JSON timings — the standard 2D mouth vocabulary.

## Recommended paths (decision input, not a commitment)

**A. Cheap A/B on the current stack (next render, ~1× render time).**
After Ollama is shut down: extend `tools/musetalk_run.py` with
`--parsing-mode/--extra-margin/--audio-padding-left/--audio-padding-right/--left-cheek-width/--right-cheek-width`
passthrough, run one render with `extra_margin=20`, `audio_padding_length_left=3`
(one-frame later onset), fp16 kept, then the tested USM pass
(`unsharp=5:5:0.8`) and re-run the mouth contact-sheet QA. Expected: modest
edge/seam improvement; mouth will likely still be soft. Value: calibrates
whether any MuseTalk knob moves the needle before bigger work.

**B. Style-preserving mouth recomposite (best effort/quality trade for this avatar).**
Treat MuseTalk (or a Wav2Lip-family warp) as a *mouth-motion source only*:
track the mouth on the original still, generate/sync only the lower-face
crop, then paste it back with a feathered alpha (8–15 px), tracked to nose/
chin, blended ~70–85%, grain-matched. This is the approach practitioners
recommend for painted plates and directly attacks our defect (MuseTalk
repainting the whole lower face). Implementation: new post-step in the
lipsync adapter or a scratch compositor first; needs the same face/mouth
detector we already patched plus a mask.

**C. 2D mouth-shape pipeline (most style-native, most new machinery).**
For an anime portrait, the "correct" solution is often not a photoreal
lip-sync model at all: drive a small set of drawn mouth shapes (closed /
half / open, optionally G/H/X) timed by Rhubarb phonemes or the
anime-lipsync node's Allosaurus→3-mouth-type mapping. Highest stylistic
fidelity, but requires mouth-shape assets and either a custom compositor
(Rhubarb route) or a ComfyUI diffusion stack (anime-lipsync route).

**D. Change the avatar or accept the look.**
If the character must stay this illustration, options A/B are the honest
ceiling for MuseTalk. If the deliverable can use a photoreal or
semi-realistic portrait, MuseTalk/LatentSync-1.5 quality improves
substantially. If a bigger GPU appears later, LatentSync 1.6 (512 px) is the
quality ceiling among open re-dub models.

## Path C implementation spec (2D mouth-shape pipeline)

Concrete shape of the "no realism prior" option for a **static portrait**
(fixed camera, no head motion — the easy case): mouth shapes are pre-aligned
overlays swapped per frame; nothing is generated, so the illustration style
is preserved by construction.

1. **Timing — Rhubarb Lip Sync v1.14.0** (DanielSWolf, MIT; ~83 MB Windows
   zip; CPU-only). `rhubarb -f json -o mouth.json --fps 25 voice.wav` emits a
   mouth-shape letter (A–F basic, optional G/H/X) per frame. Lip-sync output
   data belongs to the user; permissive for commercial use. Not currently
   installed — download from GitHub releases. Alternative: Allosaurus
   phonemes → custom 3-mouth mapping (what `anime-lipsync` does), heavier.
2. **Shape vocabulary** — start with 4 assets mapped from Rhubarb letters:
   `X/A→closed` (the still's own mouth = rest), `B/C→half-open`, `D/E→open`,
   `F→pucker`; G/H later if needed. The letter→asset mapping is ours to
   define in the compositor.
3. **Assets** — three routes, best→fastest:
   - *User-drawn* mouth PNGs (transparent, aligned to the portrait's mouth) —
     best style match, ~3–6 shapes.
   - *Procedural prototype* — DWpose already gives 68 face landmarks on this
     avatar; mouth landmarks (~idx 48–67) give exact lip geometry. Synthesize
     open/half/pucker variants in PIL/cv2 (dark interior fill + preserved
     lip line) — validates the whole pipeline today with zero new art.
   - *ComfyUI inpaint variants* — needs ComfyUI up; consistency risk.
4. **Compositor** (~50 lines): still + per-frame shape swap at fixed coords,
   feathered alpha, optional 1–2-frame crossfade between shapes, ffmpeg
   rawvideo pipe + audio copy → mp4. No tracking needed for a still.
5. **Integration**: standalone script → `output/video/`; if it wins visually,
   register as a `lipsync-shapes` engine option beside MuseTalk in
   `config/lipsync.json`/adapter.
6. **Risks**: shape quality is the whole ballgame; Rhubarb is speech-tuned —
   verify onset alignment on the cloned vocal (held notes may need manual
   frames); procedural mouths may read cheap until replaced with drawn art.

## Open questions

- Does `audio_padding_length_left/right` actually fix the ~0.12 s mouth-lead
  we measured, or is that an artifact of our envelope-alignment proxy?
- Is `extra_margin=20` net-positive on *this* portrait (more chin context vs
  more synthesized skin)? Needs the A/B render to answer.
- For path B, which mouth-motion source: MuseTalk lower-face crop (already
  integrated) or a lighter warp model? MuseTalk output is already mouth-
  synced; the work is the recomposite, not a new model.
- Should the lipsync adapter grow a `post_process` section (USM/GFPGAN/
  region masks) once a winner is chosen, or stay render-only?
