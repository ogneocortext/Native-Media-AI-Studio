# A Professional Dungeon Track, Measured (2026)

Production — companion to `tracker-pattern-craft-2026.md`. That doc's
density figures were derivations from tutorials, and it said so. This
one is a measurement: a single professional dungeon track read
measure-by-measure from notation and reduced to numbers the generator
and the lint can use. Method: private study of a published
transcription viewed in the owner's MuseScore account (viewing only,
nothing downloaded or redistributed); the figures below are analysis,
in this project's own words. One score is one data point — a
reference profile, not a corpus average.

Source: "Dungeon of Shadows," Koji Kondo (The Legend of Zelda: A Link
to the Past), transcription by Luiishu, MuseScore. Two staves, three
strands (treble dyad top/middle + bass staff). 30 bars, tempo ~140.

## The measured profile

- **Form**: 4-bar introduction heard once; bars 5–30 are the repeat
  loop (26 bars). The last two bars restate bars 3–4 literally and
  the repeat returns to bar 5 — the intro is excluded from the loop,
  and the final sonority is an unresolved add9 colour, so the loop
  point does not feel like an ending.
- **Ostinato**: two-note dyads as semiquavers, accents on each group
  of four. The rhythm NEVER changes in 30 bars. All variation is
  pitch: literal 4-bar repeats, then transposition, then a ~16-bar
  settled section alternating just two dyads.
- **The top voice barely moves**: one pitch per bar (motion only at
  barlines), total range a perfect 5th across the whole piece. The
  middle voice parks on a single pitch (C) for the entire second
  half; the settled section's engine is a semitone neighbour (E vs
  Eb) oscillating over that pedal.
- **Harmonic rhythm**: one chord per bar in the treble — but the
  bass TIES across barlines, so bass changes lag the treble by up
  to a bar. Harmonic blur is a device, not sloppiness.
- **Bass discipline**: silent for the 4-bar intro; enters exactly at
  the loop start. Long tied values (half/whole) are the norm;
  quarter-note triplets appear as punctuation in only 4 of 26 loop
  bars; eighth figures live in second halves of bars. Range roughly
  C2–C4 — wide but sparse. The bass staff's upper voice is a slow
  counter-melody of tied notes, about one pitch per bar.
- **Dynamics/articulation as arrangement**: ONE dynamic arc (pp
  crescendo to mf in bar 1, then mf held); the only other marking
  is a bass-only diminuendo near the end. Accents run bars 1–20,
  are REMOVED for bars 21–26 (a six-bar trough created by deleting
  articulation, not by changing notes), and return for the close.
- **Dissonance by collision**: the opening dyad is a tritone; at
  structural joints the bass is set deliberately against the treble
  (an Eb sonority under the tritone dyad at the loop start; a major
  chord over a tied flat at the transposition point), resolved by
  continuation rather than avoided.

## What this corrects in our practice

1. **Tempo axis**: dungeon menace here is ~140 BPM and QUIET (pp to
   mf), not slow. The sandbox dungeon study at 88 BPM was on the
   wrong axis entirely; slow + sparse reads as ambient, fast +
   quiet reads as dungeon.
2. **Coverage is not density**: the treble's row coverage is 1.00
   for 30 bars while its pitch-change rate is ~1 per bar. The lint
   measured coverage and called busy patterns bad and empty ones
   good; the professional figure is full coverage at a near-zero
   information rate. NEW METRIC (report-first): pitch changes per
   bar per voice. Ostinato-class leads sit near 1.0.
3. **Lead range**: a 5th for a whole piece. Our rejected leads
   ranged 16–21 semitones per song. Dungeon-class lead guidance:
   within-pattern range ~a 5th, motion at barline rate is
   legitimate professional practice.
4. **Repetition depth**: whole 4-bar groups repeat literally, and
   a two-dyad alternation sustains ~16 bars. Our motif law (~75%
   literal) understates professional economy by a wide margin.
5. **Bass**: late entry (at the loop start, not bar 1), ties over
   barlines, triplet punctuation rationed to ~15% of bars. A
   walking bass in every bar is busier than the professional
   reference by an order of magnitude.
6. **Arrangement without new material**: the mid-piece trough is
   articulation deletion; the dynamic arc is a single crescendo.
   Flatness is fixed by accent/dynamic architecture, not by adding
   sections or notes.

## Honesty box

Single transcription, and a transcription is an arranger's reading
of the original, not the original sequence data. A few of the
deepest bass octaves sat at the legibility limit of the viewer and
are reported as approximate. Treat every figure as a reference
point to compose against, not a norm — the profile earns gate
status in the lint only when more scores agree with it.
