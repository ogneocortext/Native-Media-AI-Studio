# Visual Storytelling Grammar 2026

> Written 2026-11-07 at the owner's direction: the library taught
> agents the *vocabulary* of shots (music-video-production's lists)
> and one production's *practice* ([[storyboard-enrichment-from-track-practice-2026]],
> rules R1–R6), but no grammar — nothing that says what a sequence
> of shots *means*, why a cut lands, or what good looks like. This
> doc is the grammar, generalized from that practice and shown in
> worked examples. It is the storytelling source for storyboard
> enrichment (T-VID-02 Step 5) and is registered as T-VID-27.

## 1. Vocabulary is not grammar

A shot list is a dictionary. Grammar is the rules by which shots
combine into meaning: what the viewer learns, feels, and expects
next. Every rule below is a *decision procedure*, not a
description — and each is traceable to evidence in the practice
doc (cited as R1–R6) or stated as a house rule.

## 2. First decision: the mode

Before any panel, choose how the video means:

| Mode | The video is… | Choose when the lyric… |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance** | the singer, present, delivering | speaks in first person, present tense, to a "you" or the listener |
| **Narrative** | a story with a place, a sequence, a change | has characters, a setting, or events in time |
| **Conceptual** | an idea or system made visible | argues, lists, satirizes, or describes a mechanism |
| **Hybrid** | performance anchored, concept/narrative interleaved | does both — the catalog's common case |

The catalog, mapped: Don't Recommend = performance × conceptual
(the system is the antagonist); Screen Name Days = narrative;
Out for Delivery = narrative satire; The Last Original =
conceptual; Human in the Loop = performance × conceptual;
Unproductive = narrative × performance (V5's place-and-character
intercut is the hybrid in practice). **The mode is board data** —
it constrains every later choice, and a board without one drifts
into wallpaper (the canon's word, [[media-anti-patterns-2026]] §5).

## 3. The motivated cut

A cut is legal when it carries one of four motivations:

1. **New information** — a place, a face, a detail the viewer
   didn't have.
2. **A musical event** — a hook entrance, a drop, a build-up's
   release (R6: the biggest visual changes land on the major
   hooks).
3. **A lyric event** — a handoff between voices (the practice
   doc's Rinse and Repeat note: vocal handoffs are coverage cues
   — cut or reframe on the handoff), a name, a turn in the
   argument.
4. **An energy shift** — the measured energy moves and the
   picture answers.

A cut with none of the four is decoration, and decoration in
sequence is wallpaper. **On-beat vs off-beat:** cutting on the
beat reads as agreement and propulsion; cutting against it
(early, late, or held across) reads as tension, unease, or
comedy. Choose per the lyric's stance — Don't Recommend's
argument with the algorithm *wants* friction; a love song
doesn't. Build-up sections are pre-marked accent points
(Human in the Loop's "YOU GLITCH" build-ups precede each hook):
the cut plan is written at the build-up, not improvised at the
drop.

## 4. Section grammar — measured, not typical

The generic mapping ("verse = medium shots, chorus = close-ups")
is a prior, not a plan. The house rules replace it:

- **The board's grid is measured** (R1) and its sections are
  **what is actually sung** (R2 — unsung sheet sections earn no
  panels). Grammar operates on the render that exists.
- **The delta rule:** the size of a visual change is
  proportional to the *measured energy delta* at that boundary,
  not to the section's name. A quiet chorus can hold a wide; a
  verse with a rising band curve earns the closer framing.
- **Cut density follows the energy curve**, with a **stillness
  budget**: every board holds some shots long on purpose. A
  held shot after density is the emphasis — if everything cuts,
  nothing lands.
- **Hook weight is measurable from the sheet** (Patch Notes'
  8-line chorus + 4-line drop hook): line counts and repeat
  counts are grammar inputs before a single panel exists.

## 5. Coverage and sequence

- **Establish → detail → reaction.** A place is shown before it
  is used; a detail is shown before it matters; a reaction
  closes the exchange. V5's intercut — wides for place, mediums
  for character (R4) — is this rule in production clothes.
- **Framing is an information role**, recorded per panel (R4):
  wide = where, medium = who, close = what it costs. Choose the
  role the beat needs; don't rotate framings for variety.
- **Performance geometry:** the character stays **off-axis** in
  performance shots (R3) — head-on address is the fake-sync
  failure's shape; off-axis is both an honesty rule and a
  grammar one (the viewer watches someone *in* a world, not a
  puppet at the lens).
- **World conformance is continuity:** every panel must belong
  to the board's place/theme (R5's rejected Chinatown panel is
  the receipt) — a panel that breaks the world breaks the
  story, whatever its individual quality.

## 6. Motif and callback

Plant one visual motif in the first verse — an object, a
gesture, a framing — and pay it off in the final chorus,
changed. The style record supplies the motif candidates (the
look's signature element: Dial-Up's away-message window,
Drift's tail-lights, Signal's single accent color). The last
frame rhymes with the first: same framing, altered content.
That rhyme is also the thumbnail's source — the video's most
compressed statement of itself (§7).

## 7. The opening, the re-hook, the last frame

- **The opening panel is a decision, not a fade-in.** It
  carries the video's strongest visual idea or its seed (the
  motif, §6), inside the first seconds — retention is a
  grammar constraint, not a marketing afterthought.
- **The bridge is the re-hook.** Mid-video is where attention
  is spent; the bridge's visual pivot (the generic table's one
  true line) is planned as a *second opening* — new place, new
  mode register, or the motif inverted.
- **The last frame is held.** The outro resolves on a frame
  worth holding (§6's rhyme), not a defocus drift — the
  viewer leaves with an image, not a fade.

## 8. Per-look cut grammars

The style system's motion character, made operational — the
board names a look, and the look brings its grammar:

| Look | Cut density | Transition character | Camera | Stillness budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Studio Console | Medium, beat-locked | Clean cuts, UI-clean wipes | Stable, deliberate moves | Medium |
| Arcade Velocity | High at peaks | Hard cuts, impact frames | Fast tracking, whip moves | Low — spend it all at the drop's exit |
| Vice Sunset (redesigned) | Low | Slow dissolves, banded wipes | Slow broadcast pans | High |
| Analog Print | Medium | Print cuts (no motion blur), jump cuts allowed | Locked or stepped | Medium-high |
| Monochrome Signal | Low | Hard cuts only | Locked; movement is the event | **Highest — stillness is the look** |
| Night Broadcast | Medium-low | Fades through black, pulse-synced | Slow push, stage drift | Medium |
| Dial-Up | Medium | Window pops, stepped cuts, typing reveals | Locked screen geometry | Medium |
| Future Gloss | Medium | Swell-matched dissolves at drops | Smooth arcs | Medium |
| Drift | Low, swung | Smear cuts on the triplet | Lazy drift, haze | High |

## 9. Worked examples

### 9.1 Unproductive (Valley Phonk), the V5 cut — by reference

The full board is [[storyboard-enrichment-from-track-practice-2026]]
§5. Three lessons it teaches: the hook carries the largest
visual change (R6, §3 rule 2); the unsung bridge earns *nothing*
(R2, §4); and place-conformance is enforced by rejection, not
by hope (R5, §5).

### 9.2 Human in the Loop — paper board (performance × conceptual)

From the lyric-library data (145 BPM, C minor, 12 sections,
39 lines; build-ups marked in the sheet). Console look, velocity
energy. Timings are structural — the measured grid replaces
them at production (R1).

| Section | Treatment | Why (rule) |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | The loop shown, not the singer: hands, screen, room — wide | Establish before use (§5); mode is hybrid — the *system* is introduced first |
| Verse 1 | Singer off-axis mediums intercut with the loop's details | Coverage roles (§5); performance geometry (R3) |
| Build-up 1 ("YOU GLITCH") | Cut density rises *inside* the section; framing tightens per line | Build-ups pre-mark accents (§3); delta rule — the change starts before the hook (§4) |
| Hook 1 ("You glitch, I bleed") | Largest change so far: new place, full performance framing — still off-axis | Musical event (§3 rule 2); hook weight (§4) |
| Verse 2 | The loop's details return, altered — one detail now wrong | Motif planted again, changed (§6); new information (§3 rule 1) |
| Build-up 2 | Density rises faster than Build-up 1; one held shot across the last line | Escalation by contrast; stillness budget spent as emphasis (§4) |
| Hook 2 | Hook 1's place, wider — the loop is bigger than the singer now | Callback with growth (§6); energy delta answered with scale, not closeness (§4) |
| Final section | First frame's framing, singer absent; the loop runs | Last-frame rhyme (§6/§7) — the argument lands without a word |

### 9.3 The Last Original — paper board (conceptual, Monochrome Signal)

Set 1 (25 lines), dry and restrained — the grammar of *less*:
Signal's stillness budget is the whole instrument.

| Section | Treatment | Why (rule) |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | One held wide: a room, a shelf, copies — the motif (the one original among copies) in frame, unremarked | Opening carries the seed (§7); stillness is the look (§8) |
| Verses | Locked shots; movement inside the frame only (light change, a hand, dust) | Camera locked — movement is the event (§8) |
| First chorus | The board's first cut-to-close: the original, alone in the accent color | One motivated cut, lyric event (§3 rule 3); Signal's single accent spent once (§6) |
| Bridge | The copies multiply — same framing, more of them; no camera response | Re-hook by content, not by cutting (§7); the pivot is the world's, not the edit's |
| Final chorus | Return to the opening wide; the original's place is empty — or the copies are | Callback, changed (§6); the concept completes itself |
| Last frame | Held, longer than feels safe | The last frame is held (§7) — restraint graded as confidence |

## 10. What the storyboard consumes

Storyboard enrichment (T-VID-02 Step 5) takes from this doc,
per board: `mode` (§2), the **cut grammar** of the active look
(§8), per-section treatments whose rationale cites a §3
motivation, the **motif** and its payoff section (§6), and the
opening/re-hook/last-frame decisions (§7). A board that cannot
name these has vocabulary; it does not yet have a story.
