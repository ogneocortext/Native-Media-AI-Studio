# Diamond Star Enterprises — logo inspiration brief
_Written 2026-11-25 by Maya, from image searches across diamond marks, star marks, and constellation marks, plus Pinterest idea boards (the owner's suggested source). Purpose: pick ONE direction, then build the keeper as exact vector geometry._

## What the reference field actually shows

**Diamond marks.** The strong ones are single-weight line geometry: one uniform stroke, perfect bilateral symmetry, no interior clutter. Two constructions repeat:
- **Container + motif:** a clean diamond outline holding one small motif at its optical center (e.g. the L'Eternal Diamonds mark, a diamond outline with an interlaced knot inside). The container reads "institution," the motif inside reads "what it holds."
- **Framed seal:** the gem inside a containing shape (circle, hexagon, diamond badge) — the hallmark treatment.
The weak ones add sparkle dots and radiating ticks; they read jewelry-shop decorative, not corporate.

**Star marks.** Most of the field is metallic-gold gradient clip art — the exact look to avoid. The credible treatments are flat: the four-pointed spark/sparkle star (jeweler's star, north star) as a precise geometric shape, and broken-outline stars (segments with deliberate gaps) as the modern variant. Four points, not five: five reads sheriff/flag, four reads jewel/light.

**Constellation / family marks.** A real genre: one large star with smaller stars around it. Two sub-types:
- Connected by hairlines into a network — reads tech/startup, risks looking like a diagram.
- Stars placed on a strict geometry (vertices of a shape, or an arc — the Paramount family portrait) — reads composed and corporate. The arc-of-stars is the classic "children around the parent" arrangement.

## The three directions

### A — The Container (parent holds the children)
Classic brilliant-cut diamond outline, one uniform cream stroke, perfectly symmetrical. Inside it, at the optical center, ONE small solid amber four-pointed star: the first child. Standing rule of the system: each child company adds its own small star inside the diamond on a fixed grid, so the parent mark visibly fills as the family grows.
- **Strengths:** takes the owner's own sentence literally ("other stars can fit inside it"); the most holding-company of the three; the container+motif construction is proven to read at small sizes.
- **Risks:** interior stars need grid discipline and a cap, or the mark gets busy over time.

### B — The Crown Constellation (family above the stone)
The diamond stays clean and empty, drawn as in the current v2. Above its crown, a tight constellation: one large amber star (Diamond Star itself) with smaller cream stars trailing in a shallow arc, no connecting lines (or hairlines only in the large-format version).
- **Strengths:** the parent/child story reads instantly; the diamond itself stays pristine; child stars are easy to add without touching the stone.
- **Risks:** constellation marks trend techy; the arc must stay tight and geometric or it drifts toward network-diagram.

### C — The Facet Star (the star is the light)
No separate star shape. The diamond's facet lines are constructed so the crown's facets form a four-pointed star in negative space: the star is what the diamond emits, drawn by the diamond itself.
- **Strengths:** the most modern of the three and the hardest to imitate; pure geometry, ideal for a vector build; nothing to clutter, ever.
- **Risks:** subtle; at favicon size the star can disappear, so the small-size variant keeps a solid amber star (the current v2 stays as that variant).

## Maya's recommendation
**A.** It is the only direction where the mark itself is the org chart, it fits a parent company whose job is to hold things, and it gives every future child company a designed place to land instead of a redesign.

## Build note (all directions)
The keeper is built as SVG vector geometry (code-drawn: exact symmetry, one stroke weight, snapped junctions), then exported to PNG at working sizes. Generation stays in its lane: concept sketches only. That is the fix for the "unpolished" wobble in the AI-drawn versions.
